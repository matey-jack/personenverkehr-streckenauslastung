
Aim of the project: estimate the operational timetables of railway lines and stations from the published timetable. Then use this data to visualize the timetable and estimate capacity usage.
Caveat: most lines also carry freight trains which cannot be estimated from the published timetables. But the operational timetables might show gaps that hint at scheduled paths for freight trains.

For the purpose of this app, the difference between the published and operational timetable is that the operational one has the passing times of all trains, even the ones that don't stop in a station. Estimating those needs two steps:
 - knowing which path a train takes
 - estimating the passing times at the intermediate stations using a simplified physical model of trains: first using acceleration/decelaration and max speeds of stopping trains to estimate distances between stops; then using the acc/dec and max speeds of the fast train with those distances to get the passing times. Both calculations also need to include buffer times that need to be estimate or assumed with pre-defined constants.
 
The estimations of the physical train parameters and buffer times need to be fitted to match the published time-table.

For selected lines, exact distance and max speed information can be given and will replace the estimated distances. It will also make our estimates of train properties more accurate by fitting against this data.  

# Visualizations

Graphical time-table for lines between two major stations. Those will include all passenger trains which pass there, even if they don't stop on any station on the line.

Standard German time-table pattern: stations on the horizontal axis; time on the vertical passing from top down.
On double-tracked lines we only show trains in one direction (buttons to select the direction), whereas for single-tracked lines we show trains in both directions, because the train meets of both directions are crucial here.

In the visualizations critical points are marked: places where the train interval (Zugfolgezeit) is smallest. A summary beneath the visualization lists all the critical points below a given threshold and thus gives an impression of the line capacity. (A lot of short critical points means the line is used over capacity, because a delay of one train would affect one or more other trains.)

# Inputs

The basic input data is a GTFS data set for all of Germany. From this relevant train data has to be extracted. Possibly be stored in a binary format to make subsequent steps faster.

Other inputs can be given optionally:
 - hints for the path of trains that are not obvious from the timetable
 - max speeds and acceleration/deceleration for specific trains (by type and line)
 - exact distance and max speed information between stations of specific lines
